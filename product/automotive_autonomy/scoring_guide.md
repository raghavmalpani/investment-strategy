# Automotive & Autonomy Scorecard

## Segment Overview
Automotive companies are transitioning from selling hardware (cars) to selling software and services. Autonomous driving and AI-powered features represent **recurring revenue streams** and new business models (robotaxis, fleet services, over-the-air feature upgrades). We're betting on the shift from one-time vehicle sales to software-defined mobility platforms.

**Why this matters:** Tesla's FSD subscription revenue compounds without additional manufacturing. GM's Cruise (if successful) unlocks robotaxi services. Ford/Rivian can sell software upgrades over the life of the vehicle. The prize isn't EV sales - it's capturing recurring software revenue and autonomous fleet economics.

## Risk Profile
**This is a high-growth, high-risk sleeve.** Scoring emphasizes addressable autonomous miles and software attach rate potential over current vehicle profitability. We tolerate regulatory uncertainty and safety incidents if the fleet data moat and software leverage story is credible.

## Universal Scoring Dimensions (Total Weight: 7.5)

### 1. AI-Addressable Autonomous & Software Revenue TAM (Weight: 2.0)
**What we're measuring:** How big is the prize if autonomy scales and software attach rates grow?

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Autonomous fleet potential:** Addressable miles/year their fleet could drive autonomously (personal FSD subscriptions + robotaxi/delivery services)
- **Software attach rate headroom:** What % of vehicles have AI/autonomy features enabled today vs potential? (Tesla FSD ~5-10% adoption, significant upside)
- **Recurring revenue model maturity:** Over-the-air upgrades, subscription pricing, fleet services vs one-time hardware sales
- **New business model optionality:** Robotaxis (Cruise, Waymo partnerships), autonomous delivery (Rivian commercial vans), software licensing to other OEMs
- **Pricing power:** Can they charge $100-200/month for FSD subscriptions? Can they take 20-30% of robotaxi fares?

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Large deployed fleet + credible autonomy path + software subscriptions scaling + new business models launching (Tesla FSD + robotaxi plans)
- **4:** Growing fleet + autonomy investments + some software monetization visible
- **3:** Fleet in place but autonomy/software revenue unclear, experiments underway
- **2:** Limited fleet or weak software strategy
- **1:** No credible path to software/autonomy revenue

### 2. Fleet Data Moats & Autonomous Miles Accumulation (Weight: 2.0)
**What we're measuring:** Proprietary driving data that trains better autonomy models than competitors

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Fleet size with AI-capable hardware:** How many vehicles collecting real-world driving data? (Tesla millions, Waymo thousands)
- **Autonomous miles logged:** Total miles driven in autonomous mode (supervised or unsupervised) - data compounds over time
- **Data diversity:** Geographic coverage, edge case collection (weather, construction, unusual scenarios)
- **Sensor suite quality:** Vision-only (Tesla) vs LiDAR (Waymo, Cruise) - which approach will win?
- **Vertical integration:** Do they control the full stack (hardware, software, data pipeline) or depend on suppliers?

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Millions of vehicles collecting data + massive autonomous miles + full vertical integration (Tesla fleet advantage)
- **4:** Large fleet building OR focused autonomous fleet with deep data (Waymo intensive data collection)
- **3:** Growing data collection, some autonomous operations, partial vertical integration
- **2:** Limited data moat, relying on simulation or third-party data
- **1:** No meaningful fleet data accumulation

### 3. Strategic Moats (Weight: 1.5)
**What we're measuring:** Durable competitive advantages beyond data and autonomy

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Manufacturing scale:** Can they produce vehicles profitably at scale? (Tesla gigafactories, legacy OEM efficiency)
- **Brand/customer loyalty:** Will customers pay premium for AI features? (Tesla brand strength, luxury OEM pricing power)
- **Charging/service infrastructure:** Supercharger network, service center density creating lock-in
- **Platform ecosystem:** Third-party developers building on their software platform (Tesla API, potential app stores in vehicles)
- **Regulatory relationships:** NHTSA track record, self-certification advantages (Tesla vs startups needing partner OEMs)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Manufacturing scale + brand strength + infrastructure moats + ecosystem forming (Tesla)
- **4:** Strong brand + growing scale OR solid manufacturing + infrastructure building
- **3:** Moderate moats, competitive with peers
- **2:** Weak brand or subscale manufacturing
- **1:** No defensible position

### 4. Execution & Regulatory Risk (Weight: 1.0)
**What we're measuring:** Safety track record and regulatory clearance for autonomy

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Safety incident rate:** Crashes per mile driven, fatalities involving autonomous features, NHTSA investigations
- **Regulatory clearance progress:** State-by-state robotaxi approvals (CA, AZ, TX), federal autonomy rule changes
- **Product velocity:** Shipping pace on FSD updates, feature quality improvements, user adoption/retention
- **Recall history:** Battery fires, software bugs forcing recalls, quality control issues
- **Management execution:** Track record on autonomy timelines (has management credibility on promises?)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Clean safety record, regulatory approvals progressing, fast shipping, credible management
- **4:** Good safety metrics, manageable regulatory path, solid execution
- **3:** Some safety concerns, regulatory uncertainty, mixed execution
- **2:** Repeated safety incidents or slow regulatory progress
- **1:** Existential safety crisis (ban on autonomous operations) or broken execution trust

### 5. Capital Efficiency (Weight: 0.5)
**What we're measuring:** Quality filter - can they fund autonomy R&D and manufacturing without burning cash?

**Sample Indicators:**
- Free cash flow generation (or path to profitability)
- Gross margin trajectory (EV manufacturing typically negative to low-teens, improving over time)
- R&D spend as % of revenue (autonomy is R&D-intensive)
- Balance sheet strength to weather losses during scale-up

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Profitable with strong cash generation, funding autonomy R&D comfortably (Tesla in recent years)
- **4:** Path to profitability clear, manageable burn, sufficient capital
- **3:** Burning cash but improving margins, capital raises manageable
- **2:** Heavy burn, frequent capital raises, profitability timeline unclear
- **1:** Distressed financials, bankruptcy risk

### 6. Valuation as Thesis Discount (Weight: 0.5)
**What we're measuring:** How much upside remains if autonomy and software revenue scale?

**Sample Indicators:**
- Current valuation vs software/robotaxi TAM potential
- Market pricing in FSD success? (Check if valued as auto OEM or tech company)
- Optionality value: Is robotaxi revenue in the model, or is market ignoring it?

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Significant upside if autonomy/software scales, market skeptical or pricing as legacy auto
- **4:** Reasonable upside, some software expectations in price
- **3:** Fair value, market pricing moderate autonomy success
- **2:** Full value, requires flawless execution to justify multiple
- **1:** Overvalued, market pricing robotaxi perfection with no margin for delay

**Trigger Flags (no score)** | — | Fatal crash causing regulatory ban on autonomous features, NHTSA forced recall of all autonomous vehicles, major battery fire epidemic, bankruptcy/liquidity crisis, loss of key regulatory approvals
