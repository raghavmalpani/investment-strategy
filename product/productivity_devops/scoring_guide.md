# Productivity & DevOps Scorecard

## Segment Overview
Productivity and DevOps companies sell software that automates knowledge work - office productivity suites, collaboration tools, development platforms, and IT service management. AI copilots and agents represent the **next seat expansion cycle**, automating repetitive workflows and expanding TAM beyond traditional per-seat licensing models.

**Why this matters:** If GitHub Copilot can make every developer 2x more productive, or Microsoft 365 Copilot can automate analyst busywork, these platforms become indispensable infrastructure. We're betting on the *magnitude of workflow automation* they can capture, not just current attach rates.

## Risk Profile
**This is a high-growth, high-risk sleeve.** Scoring emphasizes addressable workflow automation and future AI ARR potential over current profitability. We tolerate lower attach rates today if the TAM expansion story is credible.

## Universal Scoring Dimensions (Total Weight: 7.5)

### 1. AI-Addressable Workflow TAM (Weight: 2.0)
**What we're measuring:** How much human labor can AI automate within their user base?

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Workflow repetition density:** What % of user workflows are automatable? (Code generation, meeting summaries, ticket routing, report generation)
- **Seat expansion potential:** Can AI agents expand beyond traditional seats? (e.g., AI SDRs calling on behalf of sales teams, AI analysts drafting reports)
- **Pricing leverage:** Can they charge per-automation vs per-seat? (Consumption-based vs subscription)
- **Greenfield expansion:** Does AI unlock new buyer personas? (Non-technical users adopting dev tools via natural language)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Massive automatable TAM (>50% of workflows), clear path to consumption pricing, greenfield expansion visible (e.g., Office 365 Copilot automating analyst work)
- **4:** Large automatable TAM (30-50%), pricing experiments underway, some greenfield signals
- **3:** Moderate automatable TAM (15-30%), still per-seat pricing, limited expansion beyond core users
- **2:** Small automatable TAM (<15%), unclear pricing model
- **1:** AI features feel like bolt-ons, no clear TAM expansion

### 2. Competitive Positioning & Data Moats (Weight: 2.0)
**What we're measuring:** Can they train better models than competitors using proprietary workflow data?

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Proprietary training data:** Do they have unique workflow telemetry competitors can't replicate? (GitHub code commits, ServiceNow ticket histories, Atlassian project data)
- **Platform lock-in:** Are AI features deepening switching costs? (Copilot trained on your codebase, AI agents with company-specific context)
- **Strategic partnerships:** Model provider relationships (OpenAI, Anthropic) vs building in-house
- **Network effects:** Does AI improve with more users on the platform? (Collaborative AI, shared workflow templates)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Unique proprietary data, AI-driven lock-in accelerating, strong model partnerships (GitHub = Microsoft × OpenAI)
- **4:** Good data moats, AI features stickier than legacy product, credible partnerships
- **3:** Some proprietary data, AI features competitive with peers, standard model integrations
- **2:** Commodity data sources, AI features easily replicated
- **1:** No data moat, lagging competitors on AI

### 3. Strategic Moats (Weight: 1.5)
**What we're measuring:** Durable competitive advantages beyond AI-specific features

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Embedded workflows:** How painful is migration? (Developer toolchains, IT service workflows)
- **Enterprise distribution:** Existing enterprise footprint for bundling AI features
- **Brand/certification moats:** Compliance requirements favor incumbents (SOC 2, FedRAMP)
- **Platform ecosystem:** Third-party integrations that reinforce lock-in

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Mission-critical embedded workflows, enterprise-wide deployments, strong ecosystem (Microsoft 365, ServiceNow)
- **4:** Important workflows, good enterprise penetration, growing ecosystem
- **3:** Moderate switching costs, some enterprise presence
- **2:** Low switching costs, limited enterprise traction
- **1:** Weak moats, vulnerable to disruption

### 4. Execution & Regulatory Risk (Weight: 1.0)
**What we're measuring:** Can they ship AI features without breaking trust or compliance?

**Sample Indicators:**
- **AI product velocity:** Shipping cadence, feature quality, user adoption metrics
- **Data privacy/security:** Track record on breaches, compliance with GDPR/SOC 2, IP concerns (code trained on proprietary repos)
- **Customer trust:** Are users enabling AI features or turning them off? (GitHub Copilot opt-out rates, enterprise admin controls)
- **Regulatory exposure:** EU AI Act, workplace monitoring laws, IP litigation risk

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Fast shipping, clean compliance record, high AI feature adoption
- **4:** Good velocity, manageable compliance, solid adoption
- **3:** Moderate shipping pace, some compliance concerns, mixed adoption
- **2:** Slow execution or compliance issues slowing rollout
- **1:** Broken trust, regulatory blockers, low adoption

### 5. Capital Efficiency (Weight: 0.5)
**What we're measuring:** Quality filter - can they fund AI R&D without burning cash?

**Sample Indicators:**
- Free cash flow generation
- Gross margin stability (AI features may compress margins initially)
- R&D investment capacity
- Path to AI profitability (is Copilot margin-accretive or dilutive?)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Cash-generative, comfortable funding AI R&D, margins stable
- **4:** Profitable with good cash flow, AI investments manageable
- **3:** Breakeven or slight burn, can fund AI development
- **2:** Burning cash but path to profitability visible
- **1:** Distressed financials, AI investments unsustainable

### 6. Valuation as Thesis Discount (Weight: 0.5)
**What we're measuring:** How much upside remains if AI thesis plays out?

**Sample Indicators:**
- Current valuation vs AI TAM potential
- Market already pricing in AI success? (Check forward P/S vs historical range)
- Optionality value: Is the market ignoring AI upside or fully pricing it?

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Significant upside if AI TAM realized, market skeptical or ignoring AI potential
- **4:** Reasonable upside, some AI expectations in price
- **3:** Fair value, market pricing moderate AI success
- **2:** Full value, requires flawless execution to justify multiple
- **1:** Overvalued, market pricing perfection

**Trigger Flags (no score)** | — | Major data breach, IP litigation halting AI features, declining user adoption of AI products, antitrust breakup risk
