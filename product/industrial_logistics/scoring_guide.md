# Industrial & Logistics Scorecard

## Segment Overview
Industrial and logistics companies operate factories, warehouses, power grids, and supply chains with massive addressable inefficiency. AI enables process automation, predictive maintenance, autonomous materials handling, and optimization at scale. We're betting on the **magnitude of efficiency gains** when AI penetrates industrial workflows, not proof of current ROI.

**Why this matters:** If Siemens can deploy AI across millions of connected machines, if ABB can optimize robotic assembly lines with vision AI, if John Deere can enable autonomous farming - the cumulative productivity gains are enormous. The question is not "does AI work in factories today?" but "how big is the prize when it scales?"

## Risk Profile
**This is a high-growth, high-risk sleeve.** Scoring emphasizes addressable inefficiency TAM and installed base scale over current AI revenue. We tolerate long sales cycles and safety certification requirements if the efficiency prize is massive and defensible.

## Universal Scoring Dimensions (Total Weight: 7.5)

### 1. AI-Addressable Inefficiency & Installed Base TAM (Weight: 2.0)
**What we're measuring:** How much waste/inefficiency can AI eliminate across their deployed footprint?

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Installed base scale:** How many machines, facilities, or assets under management? (Siemens: millions of connected devices, John Deere: hundreds of thousands of tractors)
- **Measurable inefficiency:** Quantifiable waste AI can attack - unplanned downtime (5-20% in manufacturing), manual inspection hours, logistics route inefficiency, energy waste
- **Pricing power potential:** Can they charge for AI-driven efficiency as SaaS? (Subscription overlays on hardware sales, outcome-based pricing)
- **Greenfield expansion:** Can AI unlock new markets? (Autonomous farming enabling smaller operators, warehouse automation scaling e-commerce fulfillment)
- **Lifecycle value expansion:** Shifting from hardware sales to multi-decade software/services relationships

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Massive installed base + quantifiable efficiency prize (>10% cost reduction addressable) + SaaS pricing model scaling (Siemens Xcelerator, John Deere Operations Center)
- **4:** Large installed base + clear inefficiency target + pricing experiments underway
- **3:** Moderate scale + some efficiency gains visible + traditional hardware economics
- **2:** Limited installed base or unclear efficiency case
- **1:** No credible AI-addressable TAM

### 2. Industrial IoT Data Moats & Edge Deployment (Weight: 2.0)
**What we're measuring:** Proprietary operational data that trains better AI models than competitors

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Sensor network density:** Connected devices generating real-time telemetry (vibration, temperature, throughput, energy consumption)
- **Data uniqueness:** Do they have operational datasets competitors cannot access? (John Deere field data, Siemens factory performance, ABB robotic motion profiles)
- **Edge compute deployment:** AI running on-premises for latency/safety (autonomous forklifts, real-time quality inspection)
- **Vertical integration:** Do they control sensors + edge hardware + software stack, or depend on third parties?
- **Cross-customer learning:** Can they aggregate anonymized data to improve models across customers? (Federated learning from thousands of factories)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Dense IoT footprint + unique proprietary data + full-stack control + cross-customer learning (Siemens, John Deere)
- **4:** Strong sensor networks + good data moat + some vertical integration
- **3:** Growing IoT deployment, competitive data, partial stack control
- **2:** Limited proprietary data, relying on generic models
- **1:** No meaningful data moat

### 3. Strategic Moats (Weight: 1.5)
**What we're measuring:** Durable competitive advantages beyond data and AI

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Embedded installed base switching costs:** Ripping out Siemens PLCs or ABB robots is multi-year, high-risk
- **OT/IT integration barriers:** Operational technology (factory floor) + IT (enterprise software) integration is complex, favoring incumbents
- **Safety certification moats:** Regulatory approvals for autonomous/AI systems create multi-year lead times
- **Service/support networks:** Local technician presence, spare parts logistics, 24/7 uptime guarantees
- **Brand trust in mission-critical environments:** Industrial buyers pay premium for reliability (no one gets fired for buying Siemens/ABB)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Deep embedded base + OT/IT integration complexity + safety certifications + trusted brand (Siemens, ABB, Schneider)
- **4:** Strong installed base + meaningful switching costs + good brand reputation
- **3:** Moderate lock-in, some certification barriers, competitive positioning
- **2:** Weak moats, commoditized offerings
- **1:** No defensible position

### 4. Execution & Regulatory Risk (Weight: 1.0)
**What we're measuring:** Safety certifications, workforce displacement concerns, and AI product execution

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Safety track record:** Industrial accidents, product recalls, liability claims
- **Regulatory certification progress:** CE marking, UL listings, OSHA compliance for autonomous systems
- **Workforce displacement risk:** Union opposition, political backlash, retraining requirements
- **AI product velocity:** Shipping pace, customer adoption metrics (how many factories deployed AI inspection? How many farms using autonomous features?)
- **Customer trust:** Are industrial buyers enabling AI features or demanding manual overrides?

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Clean safety record, certifications progressing smoothly, strong customer adoption, workforce transition managed well
- **4:** Good safety metrics, manageable regulatory path, solid AI adoption
- **3:** Some safety concerns, regulatory delays, mixed customer uptake
- **2:** Repeated safety incidents or slow certification progress
- **1:** Existential safety/regulatory crisis or broken customer trust

### 5. Capital Efficiency (Weight: 0.5)
**What we're measuring:** Quality filter - can they fund AI R&D and industrial capex?

**Sample Indicators:**
- Free cash flow generation
- Gross margin stability (industrial hardware typically 30-50%, software overlays 70-90%)
- R&D spend as % of revenue
- Capex intensity (manufacturing facilities, service infrastructure)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** High-margin cash generation, comfortable funding AI R&D (mature industrial software/services players)
- **4:** Profitable with good cash flow, can invest in AI
- **3:** Breakeven or moderate margins, AI investments manageable
- **2:** Burning cash but path to profitability visible
- **1:** Distressed financials, cannot fund transition

### 6. Valuation as Thesis Discount (Weight: 0.5)
**What we're measuring:** How much upside remains if AI scales across installed base?

**Sample Indicators:**
- Current valuation vs addressable efficiency TAM (what's the NPV of 10% downtime reduction across millions of machines?)
- Market pricing in software/SaaS transition? (Check if valued as industrials or software multiples)
- Optionality value: New business models (outcome-based pricing, autonomous services) not in model

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Significant upside if AI efficiency scales, market pricing as legacy industrial
- **4:** Reasonable upside, some software transition expectations in price
- **3:** Fair value, market pricing moderate AI success
- **2:** Full value, requires flawless execution
- **1:** Overvalued, market pricing perfection

**Trigger Flags (no score)** | — | Major industrial accident involving AI/autonomous systems, regulatory ban on automation features, workforce strikes/political backlash forcing AI rollback, cybersecurity breach shutting down operations

## Usage Notes
- Update installed base metrics (connected devices, software subscriptions) quarterly
- Monitor safety certifications and industrial accident reports
- Track customer case studies showing quantifiable efficiency gains (% downtime reduction, energy savings)
- Workforce displacement trigger flags should cap exposure if political/union resistance escalates
- Distinguish between pilot projects and scaled deployments when scoring AI adoption
