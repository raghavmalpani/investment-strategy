# Healthcare & Regulated Scorecard

## Segment Overview
Healthcare AI companies deploy clinical decision support, diagnostics, surgical robotics, and telemedicine platforms. Successful products must clear **FDA approval and reimbursement hurdles**, but once approved, they gain durable regulatory moats. We're betting on the **addressable clinical workflow inefficiency** and the magnitude of outcome improvements AI can deliver, not just current revenue.

**Why this matters:** If AI can read radiology scans faster with fewer false negatives, if surgical robots can reduce complication rates, if remote monitoring can prevent hospital readmissions - the healthcare system will pay for proven outcomes. The regulatory barrier is high, but it also blocks fast-follower competition once you're approved.

## Risk Profile
**This is a high-growth, high-risk sleeve.** Scoring emphasizes addressable clinical TAM and regulatory moat potential over current FDA approvals. We tolerate long development cycles and reimbursement uncertainty if the outcome improvement is massive and the approval creates a durable moat.

## Universal Scoring Dimensions (Total Weight: 7.5)

### 1. AI-Addressable Clinical Workflow & Outcome Improvement TAM (Weight: 2.0)
**What we're measuring:** How much clinical inefficiency or poor outcomes can AI fix at scale?

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Clinical workflow addressable:** Which workflows can AI automate or augment? (Radiology reads, pathology analysis, drug discovery, surgery precision, remote monitoring, administrative burden)
- **Outcome improvement magnitude:** Can AI measurably improve clinical outcomes? (Diagnostic accuracy, complication rates, readmission prevention, treatment personalization)
- **Market size if outcomes prove:** What's the TAM if AI becomes standard-of-care? (All radiology reads, all surgeries of certain types, all diabetic patients on monitoring)
- **Pricing power from outcomes:** Can they capture value through bundled reimbursement, risk-sharing contracts, or premium pricing for better results?
- **Greenfield expansion:** Does AI enable new clinical models? (Telemedicine scaling to underserved areas, at-home diagnostics, AI-enabled primary care)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Massive addressable workflow + proven outcome improvements + clear path to standard-of-care adoption (Intuitive Surgical Da Vinci in certain procedures)
- **4:** Large clinical TAM + promising outcome data + reimbursement path forming
- **3:** Moderate TAM + early clinical evidence + reimbursement unclear
- **2:** Limited workflow addressable or weak outcome proof
- **1:** No credible clinical improvement case

### 2. Competitive Positioning & Clinical Data Moats (Weight: 2.0)
**What we're measuring:** Proprietary clinical data that trains better AI models and regulatory approvals that block competition

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Clinical dataset uniqueness:** Do they have patient data competitors cannot access? (Surgical robot telemetry from thousands of procedures, radiology reads paired with outcomes)
- **Regulatory approval lead time:** How long would it take a competitor to replicate FDA clearance? (De novo vs 510(k) pathways, clinical trial requirements)
- **Real-world evidence accumulation:** Are they building longitudinal outcomes databases that improve models over time? (Intuitive Surgical procedure data, continuous glucose monitors)
- **Strategic partnerships:** Health system integrations, payer contracts, EHR interoperability locking in data access
- **Network effects from clinical adoption:** Does the model get better as more physicians use it? (Feedback loops from clinical corrections)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Unique clinical datasets + multi-year regulatory lead + real-world evidence flywheel + deep health system integration (Intuitive Surgical, established diagnostics AI with FDA approvals)
- **4:** Strong clinical data moat + meaningful regulatory barrier + growing partnerships
- **3:** Decent datasets + some regulatory approvals + competitive with peers
- **2:** Weak data moat or regulatory lead easily replicated
- **1:** Commodity data, no regulatory protection

### 3. Strategic Moats (Weight: 1.5)
**What we're measuring:** Durable competitive advantages beyond clinical data and regulatory approvals

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Installed base switching costs:** Embedded surgical robots, integrated diagnostics workflows, physician training investments
- **Reimbursement code lock-in:** CPT codes tied to their specific technology (vs generic procedure codes)
- **Clinical workflow integration:** Seamless EHR integration, OR/hospital infrastructure dependencies
- **Brand trust in clinical settings:** Physician/hospital preference for proven, trusted brands over new entrants
- **Supply/service networks:** Consumables revenue streams (surgical instruments), technical support requirements

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Deep embedded workflows + proprietary reimbursement codes + consumables revenue + trusted brand (Intuitive Surgical Da Vinci ecosystem)
- **4:** Strong integration + good reimbursement positioning + meaningful switching costs
- **3:** Moderate lock-in, competitive clinical positioning
- **2:** Weak moats, easily displaced by better technology
- **1:** No defensible position

### 4. Execution & Regulatory Risk (Weight: 1.0)
**What we're measuring:** FDA approval pipeline, reimbursement clearance, and clinical adoption execution

**Sample Indicators:**
- **FDA approval track record:** Success rate on clearances, clinical trial execution, post-market surveillance issues
- **Reimbursement coverage:** CMS/private payer coverage decisions, adequate payment rates vs cost to deliver
- **Clinical adoption velocity:** Physician uptake, hospital purchasing decisions, patient acceptance
- **Safety/efficacy incidents:** Recalls, black box warnings, clinical outcome failures
- **Malpractice/liability exposure:** Legal risk from AI errors (missed diagnoses, surgical complications)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Strong FDA track record, favorable reimbursement, rapid clinical adoption, clean safety profile
- **4:** Good regulatory execution, reimbursement progressing, solid adoption
- **3:** Some regulatory delays, reimbursement uncertain, mixed adoption
- **2:** Repeated FDA setbacks or reimbursement denials
- **1:** Existential regulatory/safety crisis (device recall, coverage withdrawal, liability epidemic)

### 5. Capital Efficiency (Weight: 0.5)
**What we're measuring:** Quality filter - can they fund clinical trials and regulatory submissions?

**Sample Indicators:**
- Free cash flow generation (or path to profitability)
- Gross margin trajectory (hardware 40-60%, software/diagnostics 70-90%)
- R&D burn rate for clinical development
- Balance sheet strength to weather long regulatory timelines

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Cash-generative with high margins, funding R&D comfortably (established players like Intuitive Surgical)
- **4:** Profitable or near-breakeven, manageable clinical trial costs
- **3:** Burning cash but sufficient capital for regulatory path
- **2:** Heavy burn, frequent capital raises, timeline risk
- **1:** Distressed financials, regulatory pathway at risk

### 6. Valuation as Thesis Discount (Weight: 0.5)
**What we're measuring:** How much upside remains if AI becomes standard-of-care?

**Sample Indicators:**
- Current valuation vs addressable clinical TAM (what's the market if AI becomes standard-of-care in their indication?)
- Market pricing in regulatory approvals and reimbursement success?
- Optionality value: Pipeline indications, new clinical applications not in model

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Significant upside if clinical adoption scales, market skeptical of regulatory/reimbursement success
- **4:** Reasonable upside, some approval expectations in price
- **3:** Fair value, market pricing moderate clinical success
- **2:** Full value, requires flawless regulatory and commercial execution
- **1:** Overvalued, market pricing best-case clinical outcomes with no margin for delay

**Trigger Flags (no score)** | — | FDA device recall or black box warning, CMS/major payer reimbursement denial, clinical trial failure on primary endpoint, major malpractice verdict creating liability overhang, adverse regulatory policy shift (AI diagnostic restrictions)

## Usage Notes
- Update FDA approval pipeline and reimbursement decisions quarterly
- Monitor clinical trial results and publication of outcome studies
- Track hospital/health system adoption metrics (install base, procedure volumes)
- Regulatory trigger flags should immediately cap exposure until resolved
- Distinguish between FDA clearance (510(k), lower bar) vs approval (PMA/De Novo, higher bar) when scoring regulatory moat
- Watch for CMS National Coverage Determinations (NCDs) and Local Coverage Determinations (LCDs) affecting reimbursement
