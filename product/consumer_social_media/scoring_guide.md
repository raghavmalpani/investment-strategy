# Consumer Social Media Scorecard

## Segment Overview
Consumer social media and entertainment platforms monetize user attention through ads and subscriptions. AI enhances engagement (content recommendations, personalization), reduces costs (content moderation), and unlocks new formats (AI-generated content, interactive experiences). The key driver is **first-party behavioral data** - engagement graphs that train better recommendation engines than competitors can replicate.

**Why this matters:** Meta knows your social graph and what keeps you scrolling. Netflix knows your viewing preferences. Spotify knows your listening patterns. TikTok knows what makes you watch 100 videos in a row. These proprietary engagement datasets compound in value as AI models improve personalization, driving time-on-platform gains that expand ad inventory.

## Risk Profile
**This is a high-growth, high-risk sleeve.** Scoring emphasizes addressable engagement upside and first-party data moats over current profitability. We tolerate content moderation and regulatory risk if the engagement leverage is massive.

## Universal Scoring Dimensions (Total Weight: 7.5)

### 1. AI-Addressable Engagement Upside (Weight: 2.0)
**What we're measuring:** How much can AI increase time-on-platform and user retention?

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Engagement headroom:** Current DAU/MAU ratio, time-on-platform vs theoretical maximum (TikTok >90min/day, Instagram ~30min, Twitter ~30min - how much room to grow?)
- **Personalization leverage:** Can AI recommendations unlock niche content that drives incremental sessions? (Long-tail creator discovery, hyper-personalized feeds)
- **Content generation upside:** AI-generated content expanding inventory (Instagram Reels AI effects, Roblox AI-built worlds, Spotify AI playlists/podcasts)
- **New interaction models:** Conversational AI companions, AI-driven social matching, interactive AI characters (Meta AI, Snapchat My AI)
- **Content moderation cost reduction:** AI replacing human moderators, scaling trust & safety without linear cost growth

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Massive engagement headroom + proven AI-driven gains + content generation unlocking new inventory (TikTok algorithm, Meta Reels momentum)
- **4:** Strong engagement potential, AI personalization improving, some content generation experiments
- **3:** Moderate headroom, AI competitive with peers, incremental gains
- **2:** Limited upside, engagement flat or declining, AI not driving measurable lift
- **1:** No clear AI engagement strategy, losing users to AI-native competitors

### 2. First-Party Data Moats (Weight: 2.0)
**What we're measuring:** Proprietary behavioral data that trains better recommendation engines

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Engagement graph depth:** Social connections, viewing/listening history, interaction signals (likes, shares, saves, watch-time)
- **Identity graph scale:** Logged-in users, cross-device tracking, persistent profiles immune to cookie deprecation
- **Behavioral uniqueness:** Does the platform capture signals no competitor has? (Social graph = Meta, taste graph = Spotify, interest graph = Pinterest)
- **Data freshness & volume:** Real-time engagement signals at scale (billions of daily interactions training recommendation models)
- **Cross-platform leverage:** Can they train one model across multiple surfaces? (Meta: Facebook + Instagram + WhatsApp, Alphabet: YouTube + Search)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Unique proprietary graph at massive scale + cross-platform training leverage (Meta social graph, Spotify taste graph, Netflix viewing graph)
- **4:** Strong behavioral data moat, good scale, some platform synergies
- **3:** Decent engagement data, competitive with peers, limited cross-platform leverage
- **2:** Weak data moats, vulnerable to algorithm competition
- **1:** Commodity data, no defensible recommendation advantage

### 3. Strategic Moats (Weight: 1.5)
**What we're measuring:** Durable competitive advantages beyond data and AI

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Network effects:** Does the platform get better with more users? (Social graph = Meta, creator ecosystem = YouTube/Roblox)
- **Content exclusivity:** Original content libraries (Netflix, Spotify podcasts), creator exclusives
- **Platform lock-in:** User-generated content archives, social connections, playlists/watchlists
- **Distribution control:** Platform independence vs dependence on iOS/Android (Netflix app vs Snap reliant on mobile OS)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Multiple reinforcing moats - network effects + content library + platform independence (Netflix originals + global scale, Meta social graph + Instagram Reels)
- **4:** Strong network effects + some content exclusivity OR good platform independence
- **3:** Moderate lock-in, some network effects, manageable competition
- **2:** Weak moats, commoditized content or engagement
- **1:** No defensible position, easily disrupted

### 4. Execution & Regulatory Risk (Weight: 1.0)
**What we're measuring:** Content safety, privacy regulation, and AI product execution

**Sample Indicators:**
- **Content moderation track record:** AI-driven safety scaling vs human moderator scandals, regulatory fines
- **Privacy regulation exposure:** GDPR, youth safety laws (KOSA), targeted advertising restrictions
- **AI product velocity:** Shipping pace on AI features, user adoption metrics (Meta AI assistant, Snapchat My AI usage)
- **Platform policy risk:** Dependence on iOS/Android distribution (Meta/Snap) vs owned ecosystem (Roblox)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Strong AI safety systems, minimal regulatory issues, fast shipping, platform independence
- **4:** Good moderation, manageable regulation, solid AI velocity
- **3:** Moderate content safety concerns, some regulatory headwinds, mixed AI adoption
- **2:** Repeated moderation failures or slow AI execution
- **1:** Existential regulatory threat (potential bans, forced breakups) or broken trust

### 5. Capital Efficiency (Weight: 0.5)
**What we're measuring:** Quality filter - can they fund content/AI investments and scale profitably?

**Sample Indicators:**
- Free cash flow generation
- Gross margin stability (ads ~80%, subscriptions ~30-60% depending on content costs)
- Content/creator payouts as % of revenue
- Path to profitability for AI features (do AI recommendations reduce churn or just increase costs?)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** High-margin ad business, efficient content spend (Meta, Alphabet YouTube)
- **4:** Profitable with good cash flow, content costs manageable
- **3:** Breakeven or low margins but improving (Spotify approaching profitability, Snap path visible)
- **2:** Burning cash but path to profitability visible
- **1:** Distressed financials, unsustainable content/creator costs

### 6. Valuation as Thesis Discount (Weight: 0.5)
**What we're measuring:** How much upside remains if AI drives engagement/monetization gains?

**Sample Indicators:**
- Current valuation vs AI-driven engagement upside (ARPU expansion, new ad formats)
- Market pricing in AI-generated content or personalization gains?
- Optionality value: New monetization models (AI companions, virtual goods, conversational commerce)

**Scoring guide:**
- **5:** Significant upside if AI engagement/ARPU gains realized, market skeptical or ignoring potential
- **4:** Reasonable upside, some AI expectations in price
- **3:** Fair value, market pricing moderate AI success
- **2:** Full value, requires flawless execution to justify multiple
- **1:** Overvalued, market pricing perfection

**Trigger Flags (no score)** | — | Government bans or forced sales (TikTok), major data breach or privacy scandal causing user exodus, advertiser boycotts, platform policy changes killing distribution, uncontrolled harmful content going viral
