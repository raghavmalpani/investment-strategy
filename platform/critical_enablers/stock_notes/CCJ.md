**Competitive Positioning (4/5):**
- Top tier uranium producer: One of world's largest, controls key Canadian mines (McArthur River, Cigar Lake)
- Supply oligopoly: Uranium market dominated by Cameco, Kazatomprom, few others - pricing power
- Long-term contracts: Utilities sign multi-year uranium supply deals, sticky relationships
- **Not 5/5:** Doesn't own reactors, just supplies fuel - power generation companies (CEG, NEE) closer to AI demand

**Strategic Moats (4/5):**
- Mining assets: Tier-1 uranium deposits in stable jurisdiction (Canada) = geopolitical advantage
- Regulatory moat: Uranium mining heavily regulated, high barriers to new entrants
- Contract book: Multi-year utility contracts provide revenue visibility
- **Not 5/5:** Commodity exposure - uranium prices volatile, no brand differentiation

**Execution & Regulatory Risk (3/5):**
- Nuclear regulatory complexity: Mining permits, environmental approvals, export controls
- Customer concentration: Utilities globally, but exposure to nuclear policy shifts
- Geopolitical: Russia supplies ~40% global enriched uranium, sanctions/supply risks benefit Cameco
- Execution solid but commodity cycle-dependent

**Capital Efficiency (2/5):**
- ROIC 4.5% - low, capital-intensive mining business
- ROE 2.7%, ROA 1.7% - weak returns
- FCF positive but thin FCF yield 1.7%
- Gross margin 25%, operating margin 12.9% - compressed by mining costs
- **Why 2/5:** Mining is low-margin, capital-intensive

**Valuation (2/5):**
- P/E 234x - extreme multiple on low earnings
- P/S 12.8x, EV/EBITDA 59.8x - expensive vs uranium spot price
- Market pricing in nuclear renaissance, AI datacenter demand already reflected
- **Why 2/5:** High premium, limited upside unless uranium prices spike further
